## 3. Relevant index treatment

- **Corteva (CTVA) is a confirmed S&P 500 constituent**, GICS Materials
  sector, "Fertilizers & Agricultural Chemicals" sub-industry, a member
  since June 3, 2019 (cross-checked source: the current published S&P
  500 constituent list). This is the single most important structural
  fact underlying the forced-flow hypothesis — a real, large, well-
  documented index (and, by extension, the very large pool of assets
  benchmarked or directly indexed to it) currently holds CTVA and will
  receive VYLR shares pro rata at distribution.
- **No official S&P Dow Jones Indices, Russell/FTSE, or MSCI notice
  regarding Vylor's index treatment was found** in sources reviewed —
  searches of S&P's own site and general news search returned nothing
  index-provider-specific about Vylor. **Per instruction, this record
  does not infer index treatment without evidence** — it states plainly
  that, as of the evidence date, **no index provider has publicly
  announced whether, or when, Vylor will be added to the S&P 500 or any
  other index CTVA currently belongs to.**
- **General, well-established index-methodology context (not Vylor-
  specific, stated as such):** S&P Dow Jones Indices does not
  automatically add a spun-off entity to the S&P 500 upon distribution;
  a new company must separately meet index eligibility criteria
  (including a market-capitalization threshold and a profitability
  screen requiring positive trailing-four-quarter and most-recent-
  quarter GAAP earnings) and is evaluated by the Index Committee on its
  own timeline, which is **often announced days-to-weeks around the
  spin-off, not necessarily simultaneously with it.** A newly separated
  company frequently lacks an independent trailing GAAP-earnings
  history at the moment of spin-off (partly due to separation-related
  charges), which can delay eligibility determination. **This is
  general market/index practice, offered as context for why an
  announcement may not yet exist — it is not a Vylor-specific finding
  and should not be read as one.**

## 10. Strongest market-could-be-right case

No strawman. At minimum:

- **Spin-off selling is widely anticipated** — the separation was
  announced a year ago (October 1, 2025) and has been in the Form 10
  public record since June 29, 2026; any index-mismatch dynamic is not
  a secret, and sophisticated event-driven investors have had months to
  study and pre-position around it.
- **Event-driven investors may already be pre-positioning** through CTVA
  or in anticipation of WI trading — meaning some of the theoretical
  "dislocation" may already be arbitraged before Atlas or any other
  discovery source could act on it.
- **Vylor may be added to relevant indices quickly** — nothing in the
  evidence gathered rules this out; the absence of an announcement
  could equally mean "decision pending, to be announced favorably soon"
  as "decision pending, likely unfavorable."
- **Natural seed/agriculture-sector buyers may readily absorb any
  index-driven supply** — Vylor is a large ($7.5B+ H1-2026-revenue),
  profitable, well-known business (not an obscure micro-cap), and
  dedicated sector investors, value investors, or index funds tracking
  broader ag/materials baskets could step in quickly.
- **Vylor's valuation may simply not yet be established, and any
  post-distribution weakness could reflect legitimate price discovery**
  (the market efficiently finding Vylor's standalone fair value) rather
  than a genuine, capturable dislocation — this record has not
  performed valuation work and cannot rule this out.
- **The theoretically constrained passive share count (§6) may be too
  small, in practice, relative to whatever liquidity actually develops**
  — the order-of-magnitude estimate in §6 is a coarse illustration, and
  actual WI/early-regular-way liquidity is unknown until it exists
  (§7).
- **The specific dates driving this record's timeline (§2) remain
  secondary-sourced, not yet SEC-confirmed** — if any detail changes
  before an official filing, some of this record's specific dating
  could require revision.

**This case is credible and built entirely from this record's own
findings.**

## 15. Answers to the six Pilot 5 learning questions

1. **Did constraint-first discovery find this before the flow?** Yes,
   in the narrowest sense — before WI trading and before the
   distribution itself (§11) — but the underlying constraint (index-
   mismatch risk) has been part of the public record for months, so
   this is not a case of Atlas uncovering a truly hidden mechanism
   early; it is a case of Atlas engaging with a known, disclosed
   mechanism before its flow event has actually occurred.
2. **Can the constrained seller actually be identified?** Partially.
   The *class* of constrained seller (S&P-500-index-tracking vehicles)
   is identified with real primary-source support (confirmed S&P 500
   membership, issuer risk-factor language). The *specific institutions
   and their exact position sizes* could not be identified with the
   tools available in this session.
3. **Can the potential flow be estimated meaningfully using free/public
   data?** Only coarsely. A structural, order-of-magnitude estimate
   (§6) is possible using confirmed shares outstanding and widely-cited,
   general passive-ownership research, but a security-specific,
   verified 13F/ETF-holdings breakdown was not obtainable with the
   free/public tools available this session — a genuine capability gap
   this record discloses rather than papers over.
4. **Can Atlas distinguish forced selling from discretionary selling?**
   Yes, conceptually and in this record's structure (§4 vs. §5,
   `IDENTIFIABLE_CONSTRAINED` vs. `POSSIBLE_DISCRETIONARY`), though the
   practical, quantified separation of the two remains imprecise for
   the reasons in question 3.
5. **Can Atlas determine whether the flow is economically capable of
   moving price before a mature ADV exists?** Not with confidence.
   This record could establish CTVA's own liquidity as a scale anchor
   and a coarse constrained-share estimate, but explicitly could not
   compare the two meaningfully, because VYLR's own trading capacity
   does not yet exist (§7) — the honest answer is "not yet knowable,"
   not a forced estimate.
6. **What did Atlas add beyond Scout?** See §14 in full — most notably,
   the primary-sourced risk-factor language, the confirmed S&P 500
