## 4. Potential entry condition (case-specific, experimental)

**Question.** At what observable post-spin valuation, if any, would CTVA
warrant further capital comparison despite dirty fundamentals?

**Definition.** Further capital comparison would be warranted only if CTVA's
traded equity value **falls to or below the low end of the downside case,
≈ $10.4/share (≈ $6.9B)**, i.e., the market prices the whole downside case,
including $1.0B of known PFAS, with no credit for anything better; **and** no
event in §7 has made the tail unboundable on the pre-event record.
For reference, ≈ **$9.0/share (≈ $6.0B)** leaves about **$0.9B (≈ $1.4/share)** of
room for a new-tail outcome against the downside case. These are thresholds
for **continued investigation**, not entry prices.

**Context against Oct 1 (uncertified).** The Oct 1 close of $12.57 (market cap
≈ $8.4B) sits **just above the top of the downside band** ($12.0). That is
roughly **5.6–6.0x** stand-alone EBITDA on an EV that includes after-tax
pension, versus the 6.5–7.5x of my downside case. The threshold is about
**17% below** the Oct 1 close. On the Oct 1 evidence alone there is **no**
valuation opportunity independent of index flows; a decline of that size
would have to come from flow, litigation, or fundamentals.

**Sensitivity to material liabilities** (per-share value at case midpoints,
667.2M shares, after-tax present value; $1B ≈ $1.50/share):

| Incremental after-tax liability | Downside mid (≈$11.2) | Base mid (≈$17.3) | Strong mid (≈$24.6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| +$1B | ≈ $9.7 | ≈ $15.8 | ≈ $23.1 |
| +$2.5B | ≈ $7.4 | ≈ $13.5 | ≈ $20.9 |
| +$5B | ≈ $3.7 | ≈ $9.8 | ≈ $17.1 |
| +$10B | equity exhausted | ≈ $2.3 | ≈ $9.6 |

**Sensitivity to operational deterioration** (per $100M of sustainable EBITDA
lost): about **−$1.05/share** at 7.0x (downside), **−$1.31** at 8.75x
(base), **−$1.61** at 10.75x (strong). At 667.2M shares.

**Reading.** At $12.57, the market already values the stock near the
downside case, so a tail of even $2.5B would put it well below the base and
close to the bottom of the downside range. The plaintiffs' "tens of billions"
would exceed the entire equity value in the downside and base cases. That
assertion cannot be tested here, and it is why a positive finding would be
fragile.

**Responsibility of the valuation.** The operating valuation is supportable
from primary evidence. The litigation tail is **not** boundable, so the
framework is conditional on the legal record rather than complete. I report
that directly, without manufacturing a probability-weighted PFAS number.

The inputs are aggregate index-asset ratios (Dec 2024 assets over Dec 2025
market caps), and I assumed float near 1. True fund-level holdings,
sampling/replication, benchmarked rather than indexed assets, securities
lending, and the Oct 5 closing prices can each move the number. **The
bracket is a fragile exploratory range of about 25–95M shares; the sign is
better supported than the magnitude, and it is not a certified forced-flow
number.**

- **Likely sellers:** S&P 500 index and sector/style funds that hold CTVA.
  **Likely buyers:** MidCap 400 index funds. **Natural absorption:** active
  mid-cap and crop-protection managers, event-driven and index-arbitrage
  desks, the MidCap 400 funds themselves (already netted).
- **Possibly already priced:** Oct 1 volume was about 88M shares (≈13% of
  shares, ≈20x average), and CTVA traded from a 14.44 open to an 11.84 low and
  a 12.57 close, so a large repositioning has already happened. No S&P notice
  earlier than Oct 1 was found. I cannot separate flow from the spin
  mechanics, the lawsuit, or the rating action on Oct 1.
- **Realism check (my general knowledge, not sourced here):** index-
  deletion price effects are usually modest and temporary. A 17% decline to
  the ≈$10.4 threshold from flow alone would be large against that pattern.

**Could the economic opportunity exist even if the flow estimates are wrong?**
Only if CTVA's price falls below the thresholds for any reason (litigation,
fundamentals, or ordinary selling). The framework is flow-agnostic. It does
not assume that such an opportunity exists: at the Oct 1 close it does not.

price test is `BLOCKED_BY_MATERIAL_UNCERTAINTY` or
`NO_DEMONSTRATED_INVESTABLE_EDGE`. Why not
`NO_DEMONSTRATED_INVESTABLE_EDGE` now: that disposition belongs after the
price test. **The prior that the test finds an edge is modest:** the Oct 1
close is above the downside band, the flow bracket is fragile, and a
17% flow-only decline would be unusual.
