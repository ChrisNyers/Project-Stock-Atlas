## 4. Alleged ownership mismatch — mechanism-by-mechanism (load-bearing)

### A. Team preference

The issuer's own risk-factor language (§9 below) frames anticipated selling **explicitly as a preference/interest question, not a compulsion**: stockholders may sell "because they are not interested in holding an investment in the Company's business" — this is **discretionary language, not structural-constraint language.** No primary evidence supports labeling team-preference selling anything other than:

**Classification: `DISCRETIONARY_PREFERENCE`.**

A real, topical amplifier exists: the Knicks won the **2026 NBA Championship** (per context/echo reporting), which plausibly sharpens Knicks-focused investor enthusiasm for MSGK and corresponding indifference toward MSGR at the moment of this spin — but this is a sentiment/preference dynamic, not a mechanical one, and is not treated as forced selling.

### B. Mandate / structural mismatch

- **EGC restrictions**: MSGR's own risk factors acknowledge EGC status itself "may make our Class A Common Stock less attractive to investors" due to reduced disclosure obligations (no requirement for full say-on-pay votes, reduced executive-comp disclosure, extended auditor-attestation transition, etc.). Some institutional mandates do avoid or discount EGC issuers for exactly this reason. **No specific holder was identified as bound by such a policy** — plausible, not confirmed.
- **Controlled-company / dual-class restrictions**: confirmed structurally real (§2) — some institutional mandates (e.g., certain index-inclusion-adjacent ESG/governance-screened funds, or funds with dual-class-exclusion policies) do systematically avoid or underweight controlled, dual-class companies. **No specific fund was identified holding MSGS today under such a policy that would newly bind against MSGR** — MSGS is itself already dual-class/controlled, so any fund holding MSGS today has, by definition, already accepted this structure; the mismatch (if any) is only newly created by MSGR's **standalone smaller size and lack of a diversified conglomerate wrapper**, not by the dual-class feature itself, which is unchanged pre- and post-spin.
- **Minimum market-cap / index-eligibility mismatch — the most concrete confirmed mechanism found in this triage.** MSGS is a confirmed member of the **S&P SmallCap 600** (joined August 1, 2023) and the **Russell 1000**. S&P Dow Jones Indices' own published eligibility policy (near-primary — the index provider's own market-cap-guidelines documentation) states that **spinoff companies from existing constituents must independently meet the full addition criteria** (for SmallCap 600: float-adjusted market cap of roughly $1.1-7.4 billion, **plus a positive-earnings screen: positive as-reported earnings in the most recent quarter and over the trailing four quarters**) — continued index membership of an existing constituent is not automatically extended to a newly spun-off entity. **MSGR's own primary financial disclosure shows net losses in both FY2025 and FY2026 (§10) — meaning MSGR would very plausibly fail the S&P earnings-positivity screen for addition even before its market cap is assessed.** If S&P-index-tracking funds (a real, if unsized, share of MSGS's ~74% institutional ownership) hold MSGS today and receive MSGR shares via the spin-off, and MSGR is not independently added to any S&P index, those funds would face an identifiable, mechanical, index-mandated reason to exit an MSGR position that is not driven by any view on the Rangers' economics.
- **This finding was not present in Scout's discovery and was constructed by Atlas from the S&P index provider's own published methodology plus MSGR's own primary loss disclosure — see §17.**

**Classification, per instruction:**
- S&P-index-tracking funds, if MSGR is not independently added: **`STRUCTURALLY_CONSTRAINED`** in principle, magnitude unconfirmed (§7).
- EGC-averse or controlled-company-averse discretionary mandates: **`ECONOMICALLY_INCENTIVIZED`** at most (a preference with a real portfolio-construction cost, not a hard rule), unconfirmed at the holder level.
- Team-preference sellers: **`DISCRETIONARY_PREFERENCE`**, confirmed as the issuer's own framing.

### C. Stub-size / "too small to bother" effect

**Not confirmed, and reasoned through carefully rather than assumed.** The 1-for-2 ratio, applied uniformly, does **not** mechanically create fractional or odd-lot share counts on its own (only true fractional remainders trigger cash-in-lieu treatment, per the tax opinion's own language). The more plausible version of this narrative is **dollar-value materiality**, not share-count mechanics: MSGR's implied standalone value (Rangers privately valued around $3.65-4.0 billion per third-party sports-valuation services, context/echo — see §11) would be a meaningfully smaller position than a diversified large-cap holding, and some large institutions maintain minimum-position-size policies that could make a small, single-franchise stub uneconomical to administer regardless of fundamental view. **This remains a plausible narrative without measurable, holder-specific evidence behind it** — exactly the caution the work order itself anticipates. No forced-selling conclusion is drawn from stub size alone.

## 5. Index / benchmark treatment

- **MSGS today**: confirmed member of **S&P SmallCap 600** (since Aug 1, 2023) and **Russell 1000** (context/echo-sourced for Russell specifically; treated as directionally reliable but not independently verified against a primary Russell/FTSE methodology notice).
- **MSGR's own primary information statement contains zero discussion of index treatment, inclusion, or exclusion mechanics anywhere in its ~780,000-character text** — a genuine, notable silence, in contrast to Vylor (which quoted its own index-fund risk factor) and Crasus (whose index treatment came from the index provider's own external reporting, at least for Nikkei 225).
- **No official S&P or Russell notice specific to MSGR/MSGK's index treatment was found or exists yet** (the spin-off has not been completed, and index decisions of this kind are typically announced closer to or after a distribution's effective date). Per instruction, **no forced-selling conclusion is drawn without an identifiable methodology or official notice** — §4B's S&P-eligibility-screen finding is grounded in S&P's own *general*, *published*, *prospective* methodology (a real rule, applicable in principle) rather than a company-specific notice (which does not yet exist). This distinction is preserved explicitly, not blurred.
- **Answer to the section's core question**: yes, in principle — S&P-index funds tracking MSGS would face a real methodological reason MSGR is unlikely to be independently added, which is at minimum a mechanical *non-addition*, though this differs from Nikkei 225's confirmed, dated *temporary-inclusion-then-forced-removal* rule. Whether existing S&P SmallCap 600 or Russell 1000 rules **require** MSGS/MSGK-holding funds to affirmatively sell any MSGR shares they receive (versus simply never being *required to buy* MSGR because it was never added) is a **materially different, weaker mechanism** than either Vylor's or Crasus's index stories, and this distinction matters: **holding an unindexed stub is not the same forced-selling event as being mechanically removed from an index you were briefly a part of.**

## 10. Fundamental-separation check — material finding

**Do NOT deep-underwrite** — high-level only, per instruction. Findings, all from the primary Information Statement:

- **Operating loss: FY2026 ≈$30.5 million; FY2025 ≈$23.3 million** — both years unprofitable, worsening.
- **Net loss: FY2026 $(26.4) million; FY2025 $(23.0) million** — both years unprofitable, worsening (a $3.3 million/15% wider net loss).
- **Revenue: FY2026 $337.3 million; FY2025 $332.0 million; FY2024 $401.5 million** — FY2024 was materially higher, consistent with a deeper playoff run that year; revenue is **not a smooth, steadily growing line**, it is playoff-sensitive.
- **Issuer's own words, quoted directly**: "We are likely not to be profitable in the near term **unless the Rangers make an extended playoff run**, and there can be no assurance that we will not incur operating losses, adjusted operating losses or negative cash flow in the future." This ties near-term standalone profitability **directly and explicitly** to an uncontrollable, on-ice sporting outcome — a genuine, business-specific, non-ownership-related risk.
- **Local media rights revenue declining**: $39.1 million (FY2025, 12% of total revenue) → $35.3 million (FY2026, 10% of total revenue), a real, already-realized ~9.7% decline, **directly traced to a related-party counterparty's financial distress**: MSG Networks failed to refinance its credit facilities (matured Oct 2024), underwent lender forbearances, and restructured its debt on June 27, 2025 with lenders writing off ~$510 million — as part of that restructuring, the Rangers' local telecast rights fee was **cut 18%, effective January 1, 2025, with no annual escalator**. The filing further discloses that "the effect of future reductions, including from a bankruptcy of MSG Networks, could be material" — a real, disclosed, currently-live counterparty risk.
- **Player compensation** (salary-cap-constrained by the NHL/NHLPA Collective Bargaining Agreement) is disclosed as "our most significant expense" — standard, cyclical NHL cost economics, not a spin-specific issue.
- **NHL national media-rights economics**: U.S. rights (Disney/WarnerMedia) expire after the 2027-28 season — a real, disclosed, medium-term revenue-renewal uncertainty within roughly two years of a completed spin. Canadian rights (Rogers) run to 2037-38 — long-dated and stable by contrast.

**Core question answered plainly: yes.** If MSGR trades weakly after the spin, the Rangers' own standalone economics — two consecutive years of operating and net losses, a profitability outlook explicitly conditioned on unpredictable playoff success, a real and already-realized related-party media-revenue decline, and a looming national media-rights renewal — already provide a sufficient, primary-sourced, non-ownership-related explanation. This is a genuinely closer parallel to Crasus's fundamental confound than to Vylor's clean picture.

## 11. Franchise value vs. public-equity economics

Testing the "scarce assets command premium value" narrative directly, without deep-underwriting valuation:

- **The gap is real and already the market's dominant narrative, not an Atlas discovery**: MSG Sports' combined public equity value (~$9.6-9.8 billion) sits at roughly a **29% discount** to the sum of third-party sports-valuation-service estimates for the Knicks (~$9.75-9.85 billion, boosted by the 2026 NBA Championship) and Rangers (~$3.65-4.0 billion) separately (~$13.5-13.75 billion combined) — this is already widely covered by event-driven/sports-finance media (§13), **not a proprietary Atlas insight**.
- **Challenges to whether this value accrues cleanly to MSGR public shareholders**:
  - **Control discount**: the Dolan Family Group's 100% Class B ownership and ability to elect up to 75% of the board mean public Class A holders have essentially no path to force a sale, merger, or capital-return event that would crystallize private franchise value — the "controlled company" designation exists precisely because minority shareholders cannot compel outcomes.
  - **Minority-shareholder / dual-class economics**: unchanged from the pre-spin structure; the spin does not by itself create a catalyst for value realization, only a cleaner *quotation* of each business.
  - **No sale catalyst identified**: no primary evidence of any contemplated sale, merger, or strategic-alternatives process for either resulting company.
  - **Cash losses**: confirmed (§10) — MSGR's own standalone economics currently lose money, meaning any "scarce asset" valuation premium would have to be justified almost entirely on asset/franchise-value grounds, not on cash-flow economics, for a public minority holder.
  - **Related-party governance**: Arena License Agreement, Sponsorship Sales and Service Representation Agreement, and aircraft arrangements with MSG Entertainment/Sphere Entertainment affiliates (all Dolan-controlled) mean a meaningful share of MSGR's commercial terms are set in transactions with related parties, not arm's-length markets — a standard, disclosed feature of the Dolan corporate family, not unique to this spin, but relevant to whether reported economics fully reflect standalone value.
  - **Illiquidity**: unconfirmed either way pre-listing, but a standalone, controlled, EGC, single-team public stub is a plausible candidate for a persistent liquidity discount independent of any forced-flow dynamic.
- **Answer to the core question**: **not established that rising private franchise value accrues cleanly to MSGR public shareholders.** The sum-of-the-parts gap is real and already recognized, but converting it into a demonstrated Atlas edge would require confidence that: (a) the market fails to price the control/minority discount correctly, and (b) the spin itself (rather than an unrealized structural discount that simply continues under a new ticker) is what closes any gap. Neither is established by this triage-level evidence.

8. **Any post-spin MSGR weakness may be ordinary fundamental price discovery** (a newly-quoted, small, controlled, loss-making, single-franchise EGC finding its own clearing price) **rather than a temporary, exploitable ownership dislocation.**

**No item is dismissed; several are independently sufficient to weaken the forced-flow thesis.**

## 14. Cheapest falsification result

**Test:** is there a sufficiently identifiable holder population that will be structurally constrained or materially incentivized to sell MSGR for reasons unrelated to Rangers fundamentals?

- **No meaningful structurally constrained holder pool exists?** **Partially true.** The clearest candidate (S&P-index-tracking funds facing MSGR's likely non-addition due to the earnings screen, §4B) is real in principle but entirely unquantified — Atlas cannot show it is *meaningful* in size, only that it is *identifiable* in mechanism.
- **Index treatment creates little/no mechanical selling?** **Largely true, and more so than initially framed by Scout.** Unlike Crasus's confirmed, dated Nikkei 225 removal rule, MSGR's index story is a *non-addition* risk, not a *forced-removal-from-an-index-you-briefly-held* event — a meaningfully weaker mechanism.
- **Team preference is purely discretionary?** **True, confirmed directly from the issuer's own risk-factor language** (§9) — this kill condition is **satisfied**.
- **Natural buyers likely absorb supply easily?** **Plausible** — real, active, visible sports-specialist and event-driven investor interest already exists (§8, §13), arguing this kill condition is **at least partially satisfied**.
- **Stand-alone Rangers fundamentals explain likely weakness?** **True** (§10) — two years of losses, playoff-conditioned profitability, a real related-party revenue cut. This kill condition is **satisfied**.

**Net result: three of five proposed kill conditions are satisfied or largely satisfied by primary evidence** (team preference is discretionary; natural buyers plausibly absorb supply; fundamentals independently explain likely weakness), and a fourth (index-driven mechanical selling) is weaker than Scout's framing implied even where a real mechanism (S&P non-addition) was newly identified. **The forced-flow hypothesis is substantially weakened and does not survive as a clean, well-isolated thesis** — closer to Crasus's outcome than to Vylor's, though for a different, preference-and-control-structure-driven set of reasons rather than a cyclical-fundamentals reason.
