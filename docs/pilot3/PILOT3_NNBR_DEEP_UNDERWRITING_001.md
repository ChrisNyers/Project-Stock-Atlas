## 1. Core underwriting question

> Can NNBR's operating transition toward higher-growth, higher-margin
> data-center, defense, and medical programs create enough incremental
> per-share economic value to overcome the recent ~57% common-share
> dilution, remaining Series D burden, capital requirements, and legacy-
> business drag?

**Answer, in short (developed below): the operating case is genuinely
strong and broader than triage established, and the capital-structure
trade nets favorably — but the specific mechanism by which the win
pipeline converts into the capacity, and cash flow, needed to realize
that value is not yet demonstrated at the rate management itself has
guided.** Growth alone is not treated as sufficient, per instruction —
§9 and §10 are where this record finds the load-bearing gap.

## 4. New-business-wins conversion — tested, not assumed

**New primary evidence found this record** (Sept 9, 2026 investor
presentation, `nn-form8xk2026x09x10inve.htm`, accession
`0000918541-26-000087`) that triage did not have:

- **"Won programs are averaging 25%+ gross margins"** — a specific,
  quantified conversion-quality data point triage's evidence set lacked.
- **Pacing: "$90 million of new wins through 8 months"** of 2026 versus
  the raised FY2026 guide of $90–110M (midpoint $100M) — **already at
  the low end of full-year guidance with a third of the year
  remaining**, a genuinely ahead-of-pace signal, not merely a raised
  target.
- **Historical FY2024 ($73M) and FY2025 ("approximately $70 million")
  actuals** (Q4/FY2025 release) show the wins metric has **not** simply
  been inflating every year — FY2025 was flat-to-slightly-down versus
  FY2024 before the 2026 acceleration, which argues against treating the
  metric as a one-way ratchet Scout or the company might be gaming.
- **Cumulative three-year total: "more than $200 million"** (Q4/FY2025
  release), consistent with the annual figures above.

**Revenue-conversion lag, cancellability terms, and customer-funded
tooling requirements are still not disclosed** in any source reviewed —
this specific gap, flagged at triage, **remains open**. Per instruction,
this record does not assume management's 25%+ gross-margin target
converts one-for-one into consolidated EBITDA margin (SG&A, corporate
overhead, and program ramp inefficiency sit between gross margin and
EBITDA margin) — it treats 25%+ gross margin as a **favorable data
point on program economics**, not a proxy for the consolidated margin
outcome.

**Answering the work order's question directly:** based on 2026 pacing
($90M through 8 months against $90–110M FY guidance) and the new 25%+
gross-margin disclosure, this record judges it **plausible, not
certain**, that a majority of the 2026 wins midpoint becomes recognized
FY2027/FY2028 revenue at gross margins above the corporate average — but
**no source reviewed discloses the conversion timeline precisely
enough to state a number**, and this record does not manufacture one.

**China-specific commentary, gathered across the 8 quarters:** "China
sales growth... up 19%" (Q3'24), "up... 20.6%" full-year (Q4'24), "up
12.5%" (Q1'25), then **"soft China volumes" (Q1'26)** — a **reversal**

softness by Q1 2026. **This is a real, disclosed, negative data point
this record found that neither triage nor Scout's hook surfaced** —
legacy China exposure is not uniformly benign across the reconstruction
window.

**Answering the work order's question directly: legacy business is
neither purely "stabilizing" nor "still capable of overwhelming" the
newer growth areas — it is a real, continuing drag (Mobile Solutions
−14.1% in FY2025, softening China volumes in Q1 2026) that the company
has so far successfully offset through deliberate rationalization
(trading revenue for margin) and the accelerating growth engines, but
this offset has been achieved through active management choices, not
because the legacy drag itself has disappeared.**

## 8. Mix-shift economics — quantified, not merely descriptive

- **Mix today (Q2 2026, management's own figure): Auto 40% / Non-Auto
  60%**, versus **Auto 65% / Non-Auto 35% in 2023** — confirmed again.
- **Segment margin differential, newly quantified (§3, §7): Power
  Solutions ~15.1% adjusted operating margin (FY2025) versus Mobile
  Solutions ~3.0%** — a **~5x** gap. This is the single clearest evidence
  the mix shift is economically, not merely descriptively, significant.
- **Expected mix 12–24 months out: not formally guided** in sources
  reviewed — this record does not manufacture a specific future-mix
  number. Directionally, continued growth in the three named programs
  (§5, §6) at their stated "near-term goals" (a combined ~$250M versus
  today's ~$155M TTM, if all three goals were hit) would further shift
  mix toward Power Solutions, but **"near-term goal" is explicitly not
  guidance** and this record does not treat it as a base case.
- **How much of the mix shift is already priced into current guidance:**
  the FY2026 revenue and EBITDA-margin guidance embedded in the $90–110M
  new-wins figure and the general trajectory of the last four quarters
  already assumes continued mix improvement — this record cannot cleanly
  separate "mix shift already assumed by guidance" from "mix shift that
  would be incremental to guidance" with the evidence gathered.

**Answering the work order's question directly: the mix shift is
economically transformative, not merely descriptive** — the ~5x segment
margin gap means every incremental point of revenue mix shifted from
Mobile to Power Solutions carries several times the profit contribution,
and this has already shown up in consolidated Adjusted EBITDA margin
expansion (10.4%→13.9% FY2024→Q2'26) even while total revenue was flat-
to-declining for most of that window.

## 9. Capex and capacity returns — the record's central, load-bearing gap

- **Guided:** "$35–40 million" annual capex, framed by management as
  "capex averaging 30 cents per annual sales-rate dollar." **Read
  plainly, this implies the guided capex is sized to support roughly
  $117–133 million of new/incremental annualized revenue** ($35–40M ÷
  $0.30) — which lines up closely with the **$90–110M FY2026 new-
  business-wins guidance range itself**, suggesting management's own
  capex framework is explicitly calibrated to the win pipeline, not set
  independently of it. **This is this record's own interpretation of a
  stated ratio, not a verbatim company calculation — labeled as such.**
- **Actual capex, reconstructed quarter by quarter:** Q1'26 $3.297M, Q2'26
  $5.228M (implied: $8.525M 6-month cumulative minus Q1) — **H1 2026
  capex of $8.525M annualizes to roughly $17.1 million, less than half
  the low end of the $35–40M full-year guide.** This gap, flagged at
  triage as a possibility, is **confirmed and unchanged** through the
  most current primary evidence available to this record — there is
  still no quarter of actual capex spend consistent with the guided
  run-rate.
- **Maintenance-versus-growth split, expected incremental
  revenue/EBITDA per dollar of new capacity, utilization assumptions,
  and payback period are not disclosed** in any source reviewed — a
  genuine, load-bearing evidence gap, **not filled by inference.**
- **Funding:** management states "2026 capex funding will be
  accomplished via cash spending and leasing" — meaning a meaningful
  share of the "$35–40M" figure may not require cash capex outlay
  directly (lease-financed equipment), which could explain part of why
  cash capex is running below the headline figure — **this record notes
  this as a plausible, partial explanation, not a confirmed
  reconciliation**, since the lease-versus-cash split is not
  disaggregated in sources reviewed.

**Per instruction, this record does not award credit for capacity merely
because it exists.** The Wuxi CNC additions and the three named growth
programs are real, but **whether NN will actually spend the guided
capital, on the guided timeline, at a return sufficient to convert the
$90–110M win pipeline into recognized revenue, is not yet evidenced in
actuals** — this is the specific, concrete gap this record identifies as
thesis-blocking (§19, §21).

## 11. Remaining Series D economics

Per the resolved triage addendum:

- **18,400 shares remaining, ~$34.6 million carrying value.**
- **Rate Adjustment Period (Aug 2026–Aug 2027): both cash and PIK rates
  reduced to 10.0%** — implying a modest ~$3.5M/year burden during this
  window if unpaid/PIK, versus the pre-transaction 14.5%+ trajectory.
- **Reverting to 15.0%/17.0% (cash/PIK) thereafter**, with a further
  +2.5%/year step-up from March 2028 if still outstanding.
- **$5.0 million discount to the aggregate "Final Redemption" price if
  the entire remaining balance is redeemed by December 31, 2026**
  (Exchange Agreement §5.4).

**Cash required for full redemption:** approximately $34.6M face/
carrying value, **less the $5.0M discount if redeemed by year-end** —
**a net cash requirement of roughly $29.6 million**, this record's own
coarse estimate.

**Does sufficient liquidity exist?** As of June 30, 2026 (pre-
transaction, per the Q2 2026 10-Q): **cash $16.5M + ABL facility
availability $35.3M (net of $11.4M in letters of credit) = total
liquidity ≈$51.8 million.** The subsequent private placement raised
~$70.4M net, of which this record estimates **~$69.0 million was likely
used for the cash Redemption tranche** (36,750 shares × the ~$1,878.5/
share carrying-value estimate ≈ $69.0M — within ~$1.4M of the $70.4M net
proceeds raised, a coarse but credible match this record did not force).
**If this estimate is directionally correct, the July private placement
essentially funded the August cash redemption on a roughly one-for-one
basis, leaving underlying liquidity for the remaining $29.6M year-end
redemption need dependent on the ABL facility and organic cash
generation — not on a further equity raise already evidenced.** **This
record cannot confirm from sources reviewed whether a post-August-5
balance-sheet liquidity figure exists that would settle this more
precisely** — the next 10-Q had not yet been filed as of the evidence
date. A genuine, disclosed, non-blocking gap.

**Is year-end redemption economically attractive?** Yes, directionally
— the Company would eliminate a compounding, senior-to-common claim at
a $5.0M discount, financeable primarily from ABL availability rather
than further dilution, per the estimate above. **Whether it competes
with the guided $35–40M capex program for the same scarce liquidity is
the more open question** — this record does not have a combined 2H 2026
cash-uses forecast reconciling capex, the redemption, and ordinary
working capital simultaneously.

**Is non-redemption a meaningful ongoing drag?** At the temporarily
reduced 10.0% rate through August 2027, the remaining $34.6M costs
roughly $3.5M/year — modest relative to $57M+ TTM Adjusted EBITDA.
**The rate reversion to 15–17% after August 2027 is the point at which
non-redemption becomes materially more costly again** — a dated,
specific, evidence-based reason management has a real (not merely
symbolic) incentive to redeem by year-end 2026, consistent with the

## 12. Capital allocation — assessed with evidence, not by leverage alone

**The sequence, reconstructed:**

1. **July 1–2, 2026:** raised $75.0M gross / ~$70.4M net via a **primary
   equity issuance at a discount** ($3.06 vs. a subsequent ~$3.4–3.5
   trading range) — diluting existing holders by ~46.6% on its own
   (24.51M ÷ 52.57M).
2. **August 5, 2026:** used that new equity capital (per the coarse
   reconciliation in §11) to fund a **cash redemption of 36,750
   preferred shares**, plus a **separate, non-cash stock-for-stock
   exchange** of 9,850 preferred shares for 5.5M new common shares.

**Net effect: management raised common equity specifically to retire a
senior, compounding, escalating-rate preferred claim — replacing a
liability that was structurally worsening (14.5%+ and rising) with a
one-time, now-capped dilution event.** This is a **coherent, intentional
capital-structure decision**, not an ad hoc or forced one — the
transaction was announced and executed as a single, dated, negotiated
package (the Exchange Agreement explicitly conditions the stock Exchange
on the concurrent cash Redemption), not a series of reactive moves.

**Was this "least-bad financing" or genuinely disciplined?** Two
observations cut in different directions:

- **In favor of discipline:** the exchange ratio (§ addendum, restated
  in §2 above) retired preferred at **fair-to-premium value, not a
  discount, to common** — management did not force a distressed,
  below-market conversion onto existing holders merely to show a
  cleaner balance sheet. The $5.0M year-end discount is a real,
  quantified incentive structure, not a cosmetic feature.
- **Against unqualified discipline:** the private placement itself
  priced new shares at a discount to the market ($3.06 versus a
  subsequent ~$3.4–3.5 range), and the **combined transaction increased
  the share count by 57.1% to solve a balance-sheet problem the company
  had allowed to compound for years** (the preferred's $75.5M of
  accumulated unpaid dividends on top of its original face value) —
  this is a real cost borne by long-term common holders for a legacy
  financing decision, not a cost created by the current growth story
  itself.

**Answering the work order's question directly: this reads as
management choosing a real, negotiated, reasonably-priced (to the
preferred holder, not cheap) de-risking transaction once the operating
turnaround gave it the credibility and equity currency to do so — more
disciplined than a purely defensive move, but not costless to common
holders, and not evaluated here on leverage improvement alone.**
