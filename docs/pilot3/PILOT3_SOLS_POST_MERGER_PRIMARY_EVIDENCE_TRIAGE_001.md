## 4. Merger-termination economics

- **No termination fee owed by either party** (primary, termination
  agreement) — a clean exit on that specific dimension.
- **Some residual, real costs likely remain**: the termination agreement
  specifies "Parent remains responsible for all filing fees incurred... in
  connection with the... HSR Act" pre-merger notification, and the
  Confidentiality Agreement survives — **modest, disclosed transaction
  costs were likely incurred** (legal, advisory, HSR filing fees) even
  though no breakup fee applies. The press release does not quantify
  these. **This record does not treat the termination as entirely
  cost-free**, per the work order's instruction not to infer hidden
  alternatives in either direction.
- **No standstill, non-compete, or non-solicit provision was found** in
  the termination agreement.
- **Management rationale (CEO, primary):** "we have great confidence in
  our strategic plan," citing execution against AI, data centers, nuclear
  energy, thermal management, and semiconductor manufacturing — general,
  not itemized.
- **Shareholder rationale (well-corroborated secondary):** termination
  followed "constructive feedback from shareholders," with both boards
  concluding standalone operation "would serve their respective
  shareholders better... at this time"; Solstice shareholders reportedly
  "expressed excitement about Solstice's strategy and growth trajectory as
  an independent company." **This is a specific, real reported rationale**
  — not vague corporate language — though it is secondary-sourced, not
  drawn from a primary SEC exhibit.
- **Balance-sheet flexibility:** the termination removed the pending
  transaction's cash-and-stock financing commitments (a ~$14.5B deal size
  per secondary reporting) without leaving new debt or obligations behind
  — **balance-sheet flexibility was preserved, not improved or worsened
  by any new residual obligation found.**

**Answering the work order's question directly:** on this evidence,
termination **preserved value and removed a strategic option** — it did
not itself create new value (no fee received, no confirmed synergy
capture), but it also avoided integration/execution risk on a large,
apparently shareholder-unpopular transaction. **This record does not infer
hidden strategic alternatives** management has not disclosed.

## 5. Buyback economics — load-bearing

**Answering the work order's question directly:** given the buyback is
unexecuted, sized at a real-but-moderate 5% of market cap, and competing
with a large, rising capex program, **this record treats it primarily as
a credible confidence signal with modest, not yet demonstrated, per-share
value creation** — not a symbolic gesture, but not yet a proven driver
either. Execution evidence (§Falsifier) would resolve this.

## 11. Market-could-be-right case

- **Merger termination removes strategic synergies** that management
  itself once viewed as an "opportunity to accelerate" strategy (CEO's
  own words) — the deal was not costless to walk away from in opportunity
  terms, even if no cash fee was owed.
- **The buyback may be largely symbolic at this stage** — unexecuted,
  first-ever, and only ≈5% of market cap; a reasonable investor could wait
  for actual repurchase activity before crediting it.
- **AI/data-center/semiconductor growth themes are already
  consensus-level narratives** across many companies in 2026 (as seen
  repeatedly elsewhere in this pilot) — Solstice's framing is not
  obviously differentiated from the broader market's enthusiasm for the
  same themes.
- **Capex burden genuinely limits FCF conversion today**, and the returns
  on that capex are unverified.
- **Specialty-materials margins could be normalizing downward**, not
  merely absorbing temporary costs — the 218bps decline is real and
  unexplained, and a reasonable investor could read it as the leading
  edge of structural margin pressure rather than a one-off.
- **Refrigerant/HFO risk, while currently favorable, is not permanently
  resolved** — one challenge was defeated, but patent challenges and
  eventual expiries (even if "into the 2040s" for some) are an ongoing,
  not closed, risk category.
- **The current price already reflects meaningful post-deal relief**
  (+~13% on termination, holding since) — a reasonable investor could
  argue the "no more deal risk" benefit is already in the price.
- **Management pursued a large, apparently shareholder-unpopular
  transaction once already** — a real, evidenced capital-allocation
  judgment question that a buyback announcement does not automatically
  resolve.

**This case is credible, built from this record's own findings, and is
not a strawman.**
