## 4. Margin compression — load-bearing, verified precisely

**This is the central finding of this deep-underwriting record and it
refines the triage record's 218bps figure materially.**

### Which figure is "the" margin decline?

The Q2 2026 earnings release headlined: *"Adjusted EBITDA Margin decreased
218 basis points to 25.3%."* Verified directly against the primary
earnings-release exhibit: **this 218bps figure compares 2026 actual
($290M / 25.3%) against a non-GAAP "Adjusted Standalone EBITDA" for Q2
2025 of $283M — a pro forma figure management constructed to estimate
what the standalone cost structure would have produced**, explicitly
labeled in the release as *"represents Adjusted Standalone EBITDA
(non-GAAP)."* This is **not** the actual, as-reported prior-year figure.

**Verified against the audited/reviewed 10-Q segment footnote (Note 15,
"Segment Financial Data")** — the actual, as-reported comparative basis —
**Q2 2025 Adjusted EBITDA was $304M**, not $283M. On this actual-basis
comparison: **Adjusted EBITDA margin fell 417bps (Q2), not 218bps; and
508bps in Q1 2026.** Management's headlined comparison is the more
favorable of the two available comparisons, not a fabrication — the
release discloses its basis — but this record **does not rely only on
management's characterization**, per instruction, and finds the
real-basis decline is **roughly double** the headlined figure.

**Direct primary-source cause given for even the smaller, headlined
figure:** *"impacted by timing of plant turnaround activity and
production incentive credits in the prior year, partially offset by
volume growth and favorable pricing."* This is a genuine, partially
comp-driven explanation — Q2 2025 benefited from a favorable timing/credit
item that made the prior-year base unusually strong — but it does not,
by itself, explain the additional ~200bps gap between the pro forma and
actual-basis comparisons, which is structural (see below).

### Segment decomposition (new finding, not present at triage)

| | RAS margin | RAS Δ | EMS margin | EMS Δ | Corporate cost, % of revenue | Corp. Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 2025→2026 | 39.31%→34.04% | **−527bps** | 20.31%→20.64% | +33bps | 3.57%→5.25% | **+168bps drag** |
| Q2 2025→2026 | 39.42%→32.94% | **−648bps** | 18.77%→21.48% | +271bps | 4.45%→4.70% | +25bps drag |

(RAS = Refrigerants & Applied Solutions — houses Refrigerants/HFO,
Nuclear, Healthcare Packaging. EMS = Electronic & Specialty Materials —
houses Electronic Materials/semiconductor exposure. Corporate cost =
"Corporate and All Other" segment-reconciliation line, sourced from Note
15 of each 10-Q.)

**Two distinct, separable forces, now precisely identified:**

1. **Rising standalone corporate cost burden** — real and cash, but
   **decelerating sharply** (+168bps drag in Q1, only +25bps in Q2),
   consistent with a one-time, lapping transition cost from becoming a
   standalone public company (new board, new corporate functions, new
   systems) that should continue to anniversary out as Solstice completes
   its first full year standalone (Oct 30, 2026). **This is the part of
   the story consistent with "temporary transition noise."**
2. **RAS segment margin compression** — real, large, and **not
   decelerating: it worsened from −527bps (Q1) to −648bps (Q2)**. This
   sits directly under the three highest-growth, most narratively
   important segments (Refrigerants/HFO, Nuclear, Healthcare Packaging).
   **No cause is disclosed for the RAS-specific compression** in any
   source reviewed (raw materials, tariffs, and mix are plausible, given
   the pattern this pilot found at SPXC's HVAC segment, but **this record
   does not assert an unconfirmed cause** — an explicit evidence gap).
   **This is the part of the story that does NOT look like temporary
   noise on the evidence gathered — it is getting worse, not better,
   across the only two comparable quarters that exist.**

**A separate, newly identified real cost:** interest expense (part of the
Adjusted-EBITDA-to-net-income bridge, not the margin figure itself) rose
from ~$2–3M/quarter (Q1/Q2 2025, when the entity carried no debt) to
$23M (Q2 2026) / $53M (H1 2026) — the direct, unavoidable consequence of
the ~$1.97B in new debt issued at the October 2025 spin-off. This does
not affect Adjusted EBITDA but is material to net income, EPS, and FCF
quality, and is a genuinely new standalone cost with no pre-spin
comparison possible.

**Answering the work order's question directly:** margin compression is
**both** — part temporary transition noise (corporate costs, clearly
decelerating) **and** part a real, unresolved, currently-worsening
signal that growth in the RAS segment specifically is currently lower
quality than the topline suggests. **This record does not find the
margin story resolved in the corroborating direction** — it is more
precisely characterized, and the more precise characterization is, on
balance, less favorable than the triage record's single 218bps figure
implied.

## 6. HFO intellectual-property economics — deepened

**New, primary-sourced refinements beyond the triage record's
secondary-sourced findings:**

- **Patent count, primary source (10-K):** "over 5,700 issued patents and
  pending patent applications worldwide," of which **"over 4,000"** sit
  within the RAS portfolio specifically — i.e., the IP position is
  concentrated where the company's own growth and margin story is
  concentrated.
- **Expiration, primary source, more precise than the secondary sources
  used at triage:** patents "covering other aspects [of R-1234yf]...with
  varying expiration dates **at least into the mid-2030s**" — roughly a
  decade of remaining protected runway from today, not the "through
  2028...into the 2040s" range cited secondarily at triage (the two are
  not necessarily inconsistent — different patents in the family expire
  at different times — but the 10-K's own framing centers on "mid-2030s"
  as the durable-protection horizon).
- **Competitive moat, primary source:** the 10-K explicitly also
  emphasizes **unpatented trade secrets and manufacturing know-how** as a
  material competitive asset alongside the patent portfolio — i.e.,
  management itself does not present patents as the sole protection,
  which is a modest positive for durability (patents alone are
  finite-lived; trade secrets are not) but also means the "moat" is
  partly unverifiable by outside evidence.
- **Litigation/challenge risk:** the one challenge found (Arkema + two
  Chinese competitors vs. EP 2546225) was **resolved in Solstice's
  favor** at the EPO — a real, adjudicated data point, not merely an
  assertion. No other active challenges were identified in sources
  reviewed. **This record does not turn this into a legal memorandum**,
  per instruction — litigation exposure beyond this one confirmed outcome
  was not separately investigated.
- **Licensing economics:** the Hudson Technologies reclamation/resale
  licensing relationship (R-448A, R-449A) is real and disclosed, but **no
  licensing revenue dollar figure** was found in any source reviewed —
  evidence gap.

**Answering the work order's question directly:** the IP position is a
**meaningful, currently real, and reasonably durable (mid-2030s
horizon) source of protection concentrated exactly where the growth and
margin story sits** — this is a genuine positive, not merely useful
protection around one narrow product. It does not, however, resolve the
RAS margin-compression question (§4) — a strong patent position does not
by itself explain why RAS's *margin*, as opposed to its revenue, is
under pressure.

## 8. Capital intensity — central, verified

- **Capex guidance, primary source (10-K):** "capital expenditures
  representing 10% of net sales" in 2025, expected to **remain at or
  above that level near-term "due to strategic and disciplined
  project-related manufacturing expansion"** — i.e., management itself
  frames this as a sustained, multi-year growth-capex program, not a
  short-lived spike. **Maintenance-versus-growth split is not
  disclosed.**
- **Two different capex figures are disclosed for the same period** — a
  real, verified reconciling difference: the Q2 2026 10-Q's segment note
  shows **$186M H1 capex (+35% YoY)**, matching the earnings-release FCF
  calculation ($461M OCF − $186M = $248M FCF, tying to the disclosed
  figure); the **cash-flow statement's GAAP "purchases of property, plant
  and equipment" line shows $213–215M gross H1 2026 (net of $2M
  reimbursements) versus $138M H1 2025 — a larger, +54% YoY increase.**
  **This record flags this as a genuine, disclosed reconciliation gap**
  (likely a segment-note scope difference, e.g., excluding certain
  corporate/other capex) rather than treating either figure as wrong —
  but notes that **using the GAAP cash-flow figure, capital intensity is
  rising faster (+54%) than either the triage record or the company's own
  non-GAAP framing (+35%) suggested.**
- **H1 capex consumed ~46% of H1 OCF** (using the $186M figure) to
  **~46%** (using $213M, since OCF also grew) — capex is a large,
  currently-stable share of operating cash flow either way.
- **Expected returns, utilization assumptions, and timing-to-revenue for
  specific capacity projects are not disclosed** in any source reviewed —
  a genuine, load-bearing evidence gap that directly limits how
  confidently this record can answer whether the capex is a good bet.

**Answering the work order's question directly ("are attractive topline
and EBITDA growth producing enough free cash flow after the capital
required to sustain them?"):** **yes, cash generation remains solidly
positive** (H1 FCF ≈$248M) **but capital intensity is rising faster than
previously characterized, is guided to persist, and is concentrated in
the same segment (RAS) where margin is currently compressing — this
record cannot confirm the returns on that capital are currently
attractive**, only that the company can currently afford to keep
spending it.

## 19. Final disposition

> ## `BLOCKED_BY_MATERIAL_UNCERTAINTY`

**Rationale.** This record does not find evidence that directly refutes
the candidate variant — organic growth remains real and has genuinely

transition noise. **`NO_DEMONSTRATED_EDGE` is not supported.**

the same pattern this pilot identified at SPXC (a management "temporary"
framing that further reconstruction showed was not yet troughing) and is
exactly the kind of unresolved, evidence-gatherable uncertainty this
disposition exists for. **`ADVANCE_TO_FINAL_CAPITAL_COMPARISON` is not
supported** on this evidence.

**No return band, position size, or capital comparison is constructed by
this record. No Stage 7 begins.**
