## 3. IAM growth reconstruction

**FACT (unchanged from triage, restated):** IAM's ARR share rose 2.3%
(FY2025 year-end) → 10.8% (FY2026 year-end) → 12.6% (Q1 FY2027) → 15.1%
(Q2 FY2027), with sequential point gains of +1.8pt then +2.5pt.

**New primary evidence on cross-sell versus new demand — MANAGEMENT CLAIM,
not a filed fact (earnings-call transcript):** CFO Blake Grayson: "the bulk
of the... percentage of ARR that we're driving is from our existing
installed base," while also noting "a very healthy NewCo... segment." **This
directly answers the triage record's open question**, at least as far as
management's own characterization goes: **IAM adoption is predominantly
cross-sell into the existing base, with a secondary but real new-logo
component.** This is a management claim, not independently verified by a
disaggregated primary disclosure.

**Important mathematical distinction this record adds — do not conflate
two different things:**

- The **point-of-ARR-share gain** (+1.8pt then +2.5pt) has been rising.
- This does **not** by itself mean IAM's own **year-over-year dollar growth
  rate** is accelerating. As IAM's ARR base grows larger, a *constant* or
  even *declining* growth-rate spread over the total company can still
  produce a *larger* point-share gain each quarter, purely because it is
  being applied to a bigger number. **This record's own calculation (§5)
  finds IAM's dollar growth rate is more likely decelerating, not
  accelerating, as it scales** — the opposite of what the rising
  point-gain might casually suggest. This nuance is treated as central,
  not glossed over.

**Broad-based versus concentrated:** >$300K-ARR customer count grew from
1,131 (Jan 2025) to 1,205 (Jan 2026), **+6.5% YoY**, then accelerated to
1,137→1,296 (Jul 2025→Jul 2026), **+14.0% YoY** — a real, primary-confirmed
**acceleration in large-customer growth**, coincident with IAM's rising
share. This is consistent with, though not proof of, IAM adoption being
concentrated in and driven by larger accounts. **Renewal/expansion
evidence, pricing/wallet-share impact, and sales-cycle-length evidence are
not disclosed** in any primary or secondary source reviewed — genuine
evidence gaps, not filled by inference.

**AI-specific adoption within IAM (§10 detail, previewed here):** no
primary source separates AI-feature adoption from IAM adoption generally;
CEO commentary about AI agents "ingesting a record volume of agreements"
is a management claim with no accompanying customer count, revenue, or
retention figure.

## 4. Core e-signature economics — the central finding of this record

**No primary source presents a standalone core (ex-IAM) growth rate.** The
10-K states only: "we expect standalone eSignature to continue to represent
the majority of our revenue for the foreseeable future given IAM's early
growth phase" — a direct acknowledgment that core remains dominant, with no
quantification.

**This record derives a coarse estimate rather than leave the question
unanswered, using only two directly disclosed primary figures — flagged
explicitly as Atlas-calculated, not company-disclosed:**

| | FY2025 year-end (2025-01-31) | FY2026 year-end (2026-01-31) |
|---|---|---|
| Total ARR | ≈$3,029.6M (back-solved from FY2026's disclosed 8.0% growth) | $3,272M (disclosed) |
| IAM % of ARR | 2.3% (disclosed) | 10.8% (disclosed) |
| IAM ARR (calc.) | ≈$69.7M | ≈$353.4M (consistent with the release's "over $350 million") |
| **Core + other ARR (calc.)** | **≈$2,959.9M** | **≈$2,918.6M** |
| IAM YoY growth (calc.) | — | **≈+407%** (small base) |
| **Core + other YoY growth (calc.)** | — | **≈−1.4%** |

**Finding:** on this coarse but directly-derived calculation, **core (ex-IAM)
ARR was roughly flat to slightly declining over FY2026** — essentially all
of the company's 8.0% FY2026 ARR growth is attributable to IAM. **This is
the single most important quantitative finding in this record.** It
answers the work order's central question with more precision than "the
data is consistent with either reading": **without IAM, DocuSign's ARR
growth over the past fiscal year would have been approximately zero.**

**This finding must be read carefully, in both directions:**
- It is a **strong, evidence-based confirmation that IAM is now
  economically load-bearing** — not a side project, but the company's
  entire net growth engine.
- It is simultaneously **evidence that the core franchise is not a growth
  contributor at all right now**, and that the total-company growth rate
  is mechanically capped by how large and how fast IAM itself can grow
  relative to an approximately flat base beneath it (see §5).

## 5. Consolidated growth bridge

**Illustrative bridge (coarse, using Q2 FY2027's disclosed 15.1% IAM share
and the FY2027 guide of 8.5–9.0% total ARR growth), holding core at the
§4 finding of approximately flat:**

> Total growth ≈ (Core weight × Core growth) + (IAM weight × IAM growth)
> 8.5–9.0% ≈ (0.849 × ~0%) + (0.151 × IAM growth)
> **⇒ implied current IAM YoY growth ≈ 56–60%**

This is a large but *decelerating* number relative to the ≈407% FY2026
figure derived in §4 — consistent with a scaling product, and internally
coherent (a product cannot compound at 400%+ indefinitely; deceleration as
it scales is the expected, not alarming, pattern).

**What would it take to reach "a meaningfully higher growth regime"** (for
illustration, ~12–13% blended, still holding core flat)?

> 12.5% ≈ (0.849 × 0%) + (0.151 × IAM growth) ⇒ **implied IAM growth ≈ 83%**

**Comparing this requirement against the trajectory:** IAM's own dollar
growth rate is estimated in §4/§5 to already be decelerating (≈407%→
≈56–60%, coarse), and management's own Q4 FY2027 exit guide (18–19% of
ARR, versus 15.1% today) implies the *pace* of further share gains
**moderating**, not accelerating, over the next two quarters. **Reaching an
~83% IAM growth rate from an already-decelerating ~56–60% run-rate, while
holding core flat, is not supported by the current trajectory** — it would
require either a fresh re-acceleration in IAM (no evidence of this in
hand) or the core stabilizing/turning slightly positive rather than merely
staying flat (also not evidenced). **This is the specific, load-bearing
uncertainty this deep-underwriting record cannot resolve from available
evidence** — it depends on data (Q3/Q4 FY2027 results) that does not yet
exist.

## 6. Profitability quality — durability test

The triage-stage trends are **confirmed durable, not one-quarter noise**,
now checked against a fifth consecutive quarter of data and cross-checked
against XBRL:

- **GAAP operating margin:** 7.9%→8.1%→10.4%→10.5%→13.4%→13.4% — six
  consecutive quarters, monotonic improvement with no reversal, the
  cleanest trend of any candidate reviewed in this pilot.
- **SBC as % of revenue:** ~20.0%→19.6%→18.6%→17.0%→17.0% — five
  consecutive quarters of decline or stability, no reversal.
- **Capex** rose YoY (six months FY2027 $71.0M vs. six months FY2026
  $52.0M, +36.5%) — a real increase, plausibly IAM/AI infrastructure
  investment, though no primary source attributes the cause. This modestly
  tempers, without reversing, the FCF-margin improvement story: FCF growth
  is coming partly from margin/SBC improvement and partly is being taxed by
  rising capex intensity.
- **FCF margin** has stabilized in the low-to-mid 30s% (27%→32%→35%→~34%),
  durable across the period even with rising capex.

**Conclusion: these trends are durable across five to six quarters, not a
single-quarter artifact** — a materially stronger profitability-quality
finding than this pilot found for MDB (noisy, non-monotonic GAAP income)
or AVGO (SBC and amortization not improving as a share of revenue).

## 7. Buyback economics — do not credit mechanically

| Period | Shares repurchased | Aggregate price | Average price/share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q2 FY2027 | 6.466M | $306.972M | **≈$47.48** |
| Six months FY2027 | 13.257M | $625.324M | **≈$47.17** |

**Remaining authorization: $2.1B** — substantial, not a program near

**This is a genuinely important, favorable finding.** The average price

the reference price used in this record's valuation checkpoint (~$66, see
§11) — implying that, at least relative to today's price, the shares

propping up a mature stock at any price" reading, and directly answers the
work order's question: **on the evidence available, repurchases occurred
at prices that look economically sensible relative to where the stock
trades now, not merely mechanically offsetting dilution.**

This does not by itself resolve the growth question in §4–§5 — it is a
capital-allocation finding, separable from the growth-inflection question,
and is retained as real evidence of value creation regardless of how the
growth question resolves.

## 9. Competitive position

Only competition material to the variant is retained:

- **Adobe is DocuSign's primary named e-signature competitor** (10-K:
  "Adobe Acrobat Sign") — the company's own filing names one specific,
  well-resourced rival directly, not a vague industry category.
- **Generic CLM/contract-analytics vendors** are acknowledged but not
  individually named.
- **A serious, company-acknowledged AI-commoditization risk exists** (10-K,
  quoted directly): "Advances in AI have made and may continue to make
  some foundational capabilities for agreements... cheaper and easier to
  replicate," naming **"large language models, data platform companies,
  enterprise software companies, 'hyperscalers'"** as potential sources of
  "comparable functionality at lower cost," and warning that AI "may
  significantly lower the cost of developing... homegrown alternatives."
  **This is not boilerplate** — it is a specific, structural risk to the
  core value proposition IAM is built on top of, disclosed by the company
  itself.
- The Q2 FY2027 earnings call (secondary transcript) contained **no
  management commentary rebutting or contextualizing this risk** — Adobe
  and AI-native competitors were not addressed when the topic came up.

**Answering the work order's question — does IAM expand the moat or move
DocuSign into a broader, more competitive market?** The evidence supports
**the latter more than the former**: IAM explicitly broadens DocuSign's
addressable surface into contract lifecycle management and agreement
analytics, a market with more, and differently-capitalized, competitors
(including hyperscalers and LLM providers per DocuSign's own risk
disclosure) than e-signature alone. No evidence was found of a
DocuSign-specific moat (proprietary data advantage, switching-cost
argument, or network effect) protecting IAM specifically — this is a real
gap, not filled by inference.

## 14. Market-could-be-right case — strengthened by this record

- **IAM is replacing, not adding to, growth** — this record's own §4
  calculation is direct, quantified support: core ARR was approximately
  flat-to-declining in FY2026, not merely "hard to tell."
- **Core e-signature is structurally mature** — the company's own 10-K
  concedes standalone e-signature will remain the majority of revenue "for
  the foreseeable future," with no growth narrative offered for it.
- **IAM's own growth rate is most likely decelerating, not accelerating**,
  as it scales (§5) — the rising point-of-ARR-share gain is a mechanical
  artifact of size, not necessarily evidence of strengthening momentum.
- **IAM adoption is predominantly cross-sell**, per management's own
  characterization — a real, if partial, undercut of the "IAM broadens
  DocuSign into new demand" framing.
- **AI is not separately monetized** and is disclosed by the company itself
  as at risk of commoditization by hyperscalers and LLM providers — a
  credible, company-acknowledged basis for "AI features are becoming table
  stakes," not a strawman.
- **Competition genuinely broadens as IAM broadens** — Adobe is a named,
  resourced e-signature competitor, and the CLM/agreement-management space
  IAM moves into has more entrants than e-signature alone.
- **Current valuation is close to, not clearly below, this record's own
  Base case** (§11–§12) — a market pricing DOCU near ~14x forward earnings
  as a mature, ~8–9%-growth, cash-generative software company may already
  be reading the situation correctly.

**This case is now stronger than at triage and is credible enough to
justify no investment** — the work order's bar for this section.

## 15. Variant dimension and durability

**Dimension:** primarily **magnitude and timing** — whether IAM is
currently large enough, and growing at a high enough absolute rate, to lift
the consolidated growth rate meaningfully within a visible horizon, versus
merely offsetting a flat core indefinitely while itself decelerating.

**Why might the market continue to misprice IAM if adoption has already
been visible for several quarters?** Two candidate answers were tested:

1. **The market is not mispricing it** — this record's own reverse-
   engineered valuation (§11) found the price roughly consistent with its
   own Base case, and IAM's ARR-share trajectory (quarterly disclosure
   since at least Q3 FY2026, five quarters of visible data) is not obscure
   or hard to find. A market with the same public filings this record used
   could plausibly have already reached the same growth-bridge conclusion
   in §4–§5.
2. **The market may be anchored to the headline ARR-share percentage
   (which looks impressively large and fast-growing) without performing
   the same core-ARR back-out calculation this record performed** — this
   is possible, since DocuSign does not itself publish the core-ARR
   figure or growth rate; an investor would have to do the same two-data-
   point arithmetic this record did. This is a real, if not provable,
   possibility.

**No strong, decisively evidence-grounded answer was found for why a
distinctive, persistent mispricing should exist** — similar in spirit to
this pilot's findings for AVGO and MDB, though DOCU's case is less clearly
resolved either way than either of those, because the underlying
core-versus-IAM data required real (if simple) arithmetic that is not
handed to investors directly. **The variant is downgraded from "the market
is underestimating IAM" to "IAM's real economic significance is genuinely
uncertain and closely balanced against a flat-to-declining core," which is
a materially more cautious framing than the triage record's.**

## 16. Falsifiers / revisit triggers

1. **Total ARR growth relative to guide** (8.5–9.0% for FY2027) — a beat
   would be the first evidence the blend is starting to inflect; an
   in-line or miss would confirm the flat/capped pattern.
2. **IAM % of ARR relative to the 18–19% Q4 FY2027 guide** — reaching or
   exceeding it would suggest IAM momentum is holding up better than the
   §5 deceleration estimate implies; falling short would confirm further
   deceleration.
3. **Core (ex-IAM) ARR, once FY2027's year-end total ARR is disclosed** —
   this record's §4 calculation can be re-run with a new data point; a
   core growth rate that turns positive would be a genuine positive
   surprise; further negative core growth would confirm the Downside case.
4. **FCF margin versus rising capex** — whether capex growth (+36.5% YoY)
   continues to outpace revenue growth, compressing FCF margin.
5. **SBC / share-count trend** — continuation of the five-quarter
   improving pattern, or a reversal.
6. **Buyback average price versus market price** — whether repurchases
   continue at a discount to the prevailing price (as in the trailing two
   quarters) or start occurring above it.
7. **Any primary disclosure naming IAM's new-logo contribution
   specifically**, or disaggregating AI-attributable revenue/retention —
   would close two of this record's largest evidence gaps.
8. **Adobe or hyperscaler-native competitive product moves** specific to
   agreement management, which would sharpen (or ease) the §9 competitive
   concern beyond today's generic risk-factor language.

No price-target-only trigger is created; §11–§13 already supply the
valuation-based reasoning required before creating one.

## 19. Final disposition

> ## `BLOCKED_BY_MATERIAL_UNCERTAINTY`

**Rationale.** This record found genuine, durable, primary-evidence-
supported quality: five to six consecutive quarters of monotonically
improving GAAP operating margin and declining SBC-as-percent-of-revenue,
a real and accelerating reduction in actual share count, and buybacks
executed at an average price (~$47) meaningfully below the current
reference price (~$66) — disciplined, value-accretive capital allocation,
not mechanical dilution-offsetting. **None of that supports
