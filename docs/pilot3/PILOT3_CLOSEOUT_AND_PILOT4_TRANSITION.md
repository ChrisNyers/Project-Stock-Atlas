## 2. What Pilot 3 proved

Pilot 3 demonstrated:

- **Broader discovery mechanisms beyond headline/event setups** —
  capital-structure cleanup (NNBR), competitor read-through (SAIA),
  installed-base expansion (TCS), and prior batches' event-driven and
  manual-discovery mechanisms were all tested through the same
  underwriting process.
- **Improved Scout opportunity quality without source privilege** —
  Batch 003's hooks (NHP, UCB, NNBR, SAIA, TCS) engaged with specific,
  economically substantive mechanisms rather than only price moves or
  themes, while receiving no underwriting advantage over any other
  source.
- **Hook Reconstruction can cheaply falsify weak hooks** — NHP's
  hidden dilutive raise, UCB's near-zero-net EPS bridge, and SAIA's
  redundant/stale read-through were each killed on primary evidence
  before a full variant was built around them.
- **Hook Reconstruction does not mechanically reject all candidates** —
  NNBR's operating hook (growth acceleration, margin expansion, mix
  shift) and TCS's installed-base mechanism (111% Elite SaaS net
  retention, accelerating Elite ARR growth) both **strengthened** under
  reconstruction rather than collapsing.
- **Priced-In One-Liner improves expectations-gap discipline** — it
  passed cleanly at NNBR (a credible, evidence-backed gap could be
  stated) and failed at SAIA and TCS (the specific mispricing claim
  could not be credibly articulated against the evidence gathered),
  directly separating "real business" from "demonstrated mispricing."
- **Atlas can distinguish company improvement from common-share value
  creation** — NNBR's Series D resolution and dilution-earnback analysis
  explicitly weighed ~57% dilution against preferred-burden relief
  rather than crediting leverage improvement alone; TCS's valuation
  checkpoint showed a real, improving business already trading at a
  full growth multiple.
- **Atlas can distinguish productive uncertainty from thesis-blocking
  uncertainty** — NNBR's Series D reconciliation was resolved and
  cleared to proceed (`RESOLVED_SUPPORTS_THESIS`); NNBR's capex-
  execution/FCF-durability gap was, by contrast, classified and used to
  block deep-underwriting promotion (`THESIS_BLOCKING_UNCERTAINTY`) in
  the same pilot.
- **Competitor read-through requires a valid economic transmission
  mechanism** — SAIA's ODFL read-through was tested directly and found
  structurally weak (opposite volume/mix trajectories, no new pricing
  information, SAIA's own contemporaneous disclosure dominant), the
  core Pilot 3 learning test for this discovery type.
- **Real business mechanisms may already be reflected in expectations
  and price** — TCS's aggregate installed-base-expansion mechanism was
  real and improving, yet FY2027 guidance had already been raised on
  the same evidence and the market had already reacted (~+11%) by the
  evidence date, closing off the specific mispricing claim.
- **Research intensity can be allocated more selectively** — NHP, UCB,
  and SAIA were each closed at primary-evidence triage without deep
  underwriting once their hooks failed reconstruction or falsification;
  deep-underwriting effort was reserved for the one candidate (NNBR)
  whose operating thesis survived.
- **Atlas repeatedly added decision-relevant information beyond the
  original discovery hook** — every closed Batch 003 record includes an
  explicit "what Atlas added beyond Scout" finding not present in the
  original hook (NHP's dilution quantification; UCB's EPS-bridge
  reconciliation; NNBR's Series D mechanics and the broader three-
  program growth pool found at deep underwriting; SAIA's own-disclosure
  vs. read-through distinction; TCS's Elite-vs-blended ARR divergence
  and RPO maturity schedule).

## 3. What Pilot 3 did not prove

Recorded explicitly, not softened:

- **No Pilot 3 candidate reached Stage 7 capital comparison.**
- **Discovery has not yet demonstrated repeatable investable alpha.**
- **Atlas has not yet demonstrated repeatable discovery before market
  recognition** — TCS and SAIA both show the market reacting to the same
  facts before or concurrently with Atlas's own triage.
- **False-negative calibration remains unproven** — Pilot 3 has no
  evidence on how many genuinely valid opportunities the narrow fixes,
  or the underwriting process generally, may have screened out
  incorrectly.
- **Opportunity half-life remains poorly understood** — how quickly a
  genuine dislocation closes once identified has not been measured.
- **Atlas has not yet demonstrated that the originally surfaced
  security is consistently the best expression of a valid thesis** — no
  Pilot 3 record tested a surfaced ticker against an alternative
  expression (supplier, competitor, sector beta, SPY, Treasury/Cash, or
  Do Nothing).

## 5. Pilot 3 discovery lesson

> Pilot 3 showed that Atlas is increasingly effective at destroying weak
> hooks, but it has not yet demonstrated consistent arrival while a
> genuine expectations gap remains open. The next learning frontier is
> not more rejection machinery. It is better opportunity discovery and
> earlier recognition.

## 8. Best-expression question — carried forward

> If the economic thesis is correct, is the surfaced security actually
> the best way to capture it?

Potential alternatives remain: another company; supplier/customer/
competitor; sector or factor beta; SPY; commodity exposure where
relevant; Treasury/Cash; Do Nothing. The surfaced ticker is the
starting point, not automatically the final expression. **No optimizer

## 12. Pilot 4 questions

Pilot 4 should ask:

- Is the selling/buying pressure genuinely non-fundamental?
- Why must the affected holder transact?
- How long might the flow distortion persist?
- Has fundamental value changed?
- Has the tape already absorbed the flow?
- Is the original security the best expression?
- Is the apparent alpha actually factor/beta exposure?
- What is the original evidence and what is echo?
- Can Atlas validate the opportunity before the gap disappears?
- Does the candidate actually deserve capital comparison versus SPY,
  Treasury/Cash, and Do Nothing?

**These are questions only — no new architecture is created by this
list.**

## 16. Research efficiency

Kill weak hooks cheaply, but do not make shallower research the
objective. Deep underwriting remains appropriate when a candidate earns
it (as NNBR did in Pilot 3). Do not produce lengthy research where
sequential evidence already killed the hook (NHP, UCB, SAIA at triage);
the price already requires the bull case (TCS's valuation checkpoint);
the event/session is incomplete; or the remaining potential edge is
immaterial versus alternatives.
