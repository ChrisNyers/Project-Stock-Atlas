## 3. HVAC margin durability — load-bearing, tested across quarters

**Reconstructed trend (primary, four quarters, not one):**

| Quarter | HVAC organic growth | HVAC segment margin | YoY margin change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 2025 | +9.0% | 24.4% | **+50bps** |
| Q1 2026 | +9.6% | 22.5% | **−40bps** |
| Q2 2026 | +18.9% | 22.8% | **−260bps** |

**This is an important finding this record adds: the margin trend has
worsened for two consecutive quarters, not troughed and begun
recovering.** Management's own attribution (CFO, earnings call): the
260bps decline was driven by "startup costs and tariffs," described as
"known items" and "consistent with expectations" — i.e., **anticipated by
management, not a surprise blowing through their own model.** CEO
guidance: Q4 margins should be "higher than Q3 as startup costs and
tariff pressure ease" — **a qualitative, directional claim, not a
quantified recovery timeline or magnitude.**

**Is recovery already embedded in guidance?** **No — and this is a
meaningful, credibility-supporting finding.** CFO, directly: "The raise in
HVAC was driven by Neptronic. That was the 25 basis points increase. The
balance of the forecast within HVAC is unchanged." **The full-year
guidance raise came from Neptronic and data-center/D&M strength, not from
an assumed HVAC margin recovery.** This means management is not relying on
the very recovery it is claiming will happen to justify its raised
numbers — a conservative, testable posture, not a promotional one.

**Answering the work order's question directly:** the evidence is
**genuinely mixed, not resolved**. The stated causes (capacity-expansion
startup costs, tariffs) are consistent with a temporary, self-correcting
explanation, and guidance does not depend on recovery happening. But the
**trend direction over the two most recent quarters has been worsening,
not improving** — management's "Q4 better than Q3" claim is a forward
assertion this record cannot yet verify. **This is the single most
important unresolved question in this record.**

## 4. Organic growth quality — reconstructed across quarters, not one

| Quarter | Total organic growth | HVAC organic | D&M organic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 2025 | +14.3% | +9.0% | **+26.5%** |
| Q1 2026 | +7.4% | +9.6% | +3.0% |
| Q2 2026 | +16.9% | **+18.9%** | +12.8% |

**Finding, directly per the work order's instruction not to rely on one
quarter:** consolidated organic growth is **lumpy, not smoothly
accelerating** (14.3%→7.4%→16.9%) — Q2 2026's strong print should be read
against this volatility, not treated as a clean new trend level in
isolation. **HVAC organic growth, however, does show a real,
multi-quarter progression** (9.0%→9.6%→18.9%) that is consistent with a
genuine acceleration, not noise, and is corroborated by the backlog
evidence below. **D&M's organic growth is highly volatile and
project-driven** (26.5%→3.0%→12.8%) — management attributes swings to
"project volumes" in navigation aids and communication technology,
consistent with lumpy, large-contract revenue recognition rather than
smooth recurring demand. **Order growth / backlog for D&M specifically was
not disclosed** in any source reviewed — an evidence gap.

**HVAC backlog (primary, earnings call): $919M, up 59% organically
year-over-year**, described by the CEO as "primarily driven by strong
data center demand." This is real, forward-looking demand evidence, not
merely a strong trailing quarter — it corroborates that Q2 2026's HVAC
organic acceleration is not a one-off. Management noted backlog is
recognized only from "formal purchase orders," not soft commitments — a
disciplined, not inflated, backlog policy, though it also means the true
extent of longer-term hyperscaler visibility beyond formal orders is not
captured in this figure.

## 6. Detection & Measurement — durability of the margin expansion

**Reconstructed trend (primary, four quarters):**

| Quarter | D&M segment margin | YoY change |
|---|---|---|
| Q3 2025 | 25.2% | +240bps |
| Q1 2026 | 27.0% | +410bps |
| Q2 2026 | 28.9% | **+610bps** |

**This is a genuinely durable, multi-quarter confirmed trend, not a
one-quarter fluke** — D&M margin has expanded in every quarter reviewed,
with the pace of expansion itself increasing. This is a materially
stronger, cleaner trend than the volatile organic-growth figures for the
same segment, suggesting the margin improvement reflects real operating
leverage / mix shift (management attributes it to "favorable product mix"
in navigation aids and communication technology) rather than a
lumpy-revenue artifact, even though the revenue growth underneath it is
lumpy.

**Can D&M offset HVAC margin pressure if it lasts longer than expected?**
**On the evidence so far, yes — this is directly testable in the
consolidated numbers:**

| Quarter | Consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin | YoY change |
|---|---|---|
| Q3 2025 | 23.0% | +150bps |
| Q1 2026 | 22.2% | roughly flat |
| Q2 2026 | 22.4% | roughly flat |

**Consolidated margin has stayed roughly stable in the 22–23% range even
as HVAC margin has declined for two quarters** — direct evidence that
D&M's expansion has been offsetting HVAC's pressure at the consolidated
level so far. **This does not guarantee the offset continues** if HVAC
pressure worsens further or D&M's project-driven margin proves less
durable than its three-quarter trend suggests, but it is real, not
assumed.

## 7. Acquisition economics

**Neptronic (the only acquisition with disclosed unit economics):**
approximately **$75M revenue run-rate**; acquired at what management
described as roughly **"12½ times"** the business (most likely an
EV/EBITDA multiple given the disclosed "mid-40s" EBITDA margins — a
precise purchase price was not independently confirmed from primary
sources and is not asserted here as exact). **Segment income margins
"low 40s," EBITDA margins "mid-40s"** — both roughly double SPXC's
consolidated ~22% EBITDA margin, making Neptronic **structurally accretive
to the consolidated margin mix**, not merely growth-rate-additive.
**Contribution:** ~$75M revenue and **$0.05–$0.06 EPS accretion** in 2026
from roughly five months of ownership — a real, quantified, immediately
accretive contribution, not a promise deferred to future integration.

**Crawford United, Thermolec, Sigma & Omega, KTS:** purchase prices,
individual revenue/EBITDA contributions, and integration costs were **not
separately disclosed** in any source reviewed — a genuine evidence gap.
Collectively they contributed the 8.5-point inorganic share of HVAC's
27.6% Q2 growth (per the triage record), but this record cannot verify
whether each is individually accretive.

**Integration costs (primary, 10-Q):** acquisition and integration-related
costs of **$4.6M (Q2) / $12.3M (six months)** — real and disclosed, modest
relative to the scale of five completed deals, not alarming.

**Return-on-capital logic:** Neptronic's disclosed economics (high margin,
immediately accretive, funded well within the leverage target range)
support "durable per-share value," not merely "maintaining the growth
rate." **The other four deals cannot be independently assessed on the same
standard from evidence available** — this record does not assume they are
equally accretive merely because Neptronic is.

**Forward capital allocation, directly relevant to future integration
risk:** CEO, on the call: "We have a lot of capacity here. We actually see
a lot of very attractive opportunities," naming an active pipeline in
Detection & Measurement, electric heat, and engineered air movement, with
"a very attractive strategic set of opportunities even over the next six
months." **More debt-funded M&A is a real, management-signaled likelihood,
not a hypothetical** — this is both a growth lever and a source of
ongoing integration-complexity risk that this record cannot resolve in
advance.

## 8. FCF quality — deepened

**Primary, 10-Q cash-flow detail, six months 2026:** accounts receivable
and other assets **−$70.4M**, inventory **−$66.5M**, accounts payable/
accrued **+$19.9M** — a net working-capital drag of **≈−$117.0M**,

**Finding: OCF/FCF roughly doubling YoY happened despite, not because of,
a large working-capital drag** — underlying pre-working-capital cash
generation is stronger than the headline OCF figure suggests, since the
reported $120.2M six-month OCF already absorbs the ~$117M working-capital
use. This is a **genuinely durable, not timing-flattered, quality
finding** — the opposite of a red flag (e.g., a one-time working-capital
release inflating FCF) that this record specifically checked for and did
not find.

**Primary:** total debt $614.7M at quarter-end (Q2 2026); **standalone
leverage 0.7x** at quarter-end (per the earnings call), **before**

sections of the 10-Q the fetch tooling returned intact — an evidence gap,
though the very low leverage level (well inside covenant-comfortable
territory even pro forma) makes near-term refinancing risk unlikely to be
a material concern regardless of the exact schedule.

**Does SPXC have adequate flexibility if margins disappoint further?**
**Yes, on this evidence.** At 1.4x pro forma leverage — below the bottom of
management's own target range, with modest interest expense relative to
cash generation, and OCF/FCF that has been durably strong even net of a
large working-capital build (§8) — the balance sheet could absorb a
meaningfully worse HVAC-margin outcome than currently guided without
approaching a leverage or liquidity constraint. **The balance sheet is not
the load-bearing risk in this thesis; the HVAC-margin trend (§3) and the
unexplained price action (§Market reaction, triage record) are.**

## 13. Variant dimension and durability

**Dimension:** primarily **timing** (will HVAC margin actually trough and
recover on the timeline management directionally claims) and **magnitude**
(is D&M's offsetting margin strength durable enough, and data-center
demand large enough, to carry consolidated economics through the HVAC
pressure period). Less about probability or duration in the sense used for
AVGO/DOCU — the underlying secular driver (data-center cooling demand) is
well-quantified and not in question; **what is in question is near-term
execution through a margin-pressure window whose length is not yet
verified.**

**Why might this expectations gap persist?** The evidence in this record
suggests a credible, non-strawman answer: **the two-quarter worsening
HVAC-margin trend is genuinely recent and not yet resolved by any public
data** — Q3 2026 results (expected ~late October) will be the first real
test of management's "Q4 better than Q3" claim. A market waiting for that
confirmation, rather than acting on management's own forward-looking
statement, is not obviously wrong to do so. **This is a credible,
evidence-grounded reason for a temporary gap to exist, unlike this pilot's
findings for MDB and DOCU**, where no comparably concrete, dated
resolution point could be identified.

## 17. Final disposition

> ## `BLOCKED_BY_MATERIAL_UNCERTAINTY`

**Rationale.** This record found genuine, multi-quarter-confirmed
business quality that the triage record could not fully establish from a
single quarter: a real, accelerating HVAC organic-growth trend
corroborated by a large and growing backlog; a well-quantified, material
secular data-center demand driver; a durable, three-consecutive-quarter
D&M margin-expansion trend that has so far offset HVAC's pressure at the
consolidated level; an immediately accretive, high-margin acquisition
(Neptronic) with disclosed, favorable unit economics; FCF quality that
held up despite (not because of) a large working-capital investment in
growth; and a balance sheet with genuine flexibility (1.4x pro forma
leverage, below management's own target floor). **None of this supports
`NO_DEMONSTRATED_EDGE`.**

But the specific, load-bearing question this record's own reconstruction
sharpened — whether HVAC margin pressure is genuinely temporary and about
to trough, as management directionally claims, or continuing to
worsen — remains **unresolved, not refuted, and not confirmed**: the trend
over the two most recent quarters has moved in the wrong direction for the
"temporary" thesis, even though management's stated causes are plausible
and guidance was not built on an assumed recovery. Separately, the
triage-stage finding of an unexplained five-to-six-week price decline was
not resolved by this deeper review either. **Neither gap supports a
confident `ADVANCE_TO_FINAL_CAPITAL_COMPARISON`** — Stage 7 should not
proceed on "the fundamentals look good on balance" alone, per this
record's own governing instruction not to assume the selloff itself
creates edge.

Both open questions have **specific, dated, near-term resolution points**
(Q3 2026 earnings, expected ~late October) rather than requiring new
methodology — this is precisely the situation `BLOCKED_BY_MATERIAL_UNCERTAINTY`
exists for.

**Stage 7 has not begun.** No return band, position size, or capital
comparison is constructed by this record.
