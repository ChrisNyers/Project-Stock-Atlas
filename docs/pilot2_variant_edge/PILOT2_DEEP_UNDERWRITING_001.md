## FIS

### A. Business / earnings engine

**[FACT]** FY2026 is dominated by one transaction: on January 9, 2026 FIS
acquired Global Payments' Issuer Solutions business (ex-TSYS, enterprise value
$13.5B / net purchase price $12.0B including $1.5B of NPV tax assets) and
simultaneously sold its remaining 45% minority Worldpay stake. **[MGMT]**
management states this "expected additional" $500M incremental Adjusted FCF in
2026, rising to $700M by 2028. **[FACT]** For H1 2026, FIS's equity-method
share of Worldpay earnings was $8M, covering only Jan 1–8 pre-sale — **[INFER]**
crudely annualized (×365/8), that implies Worldpay contributed on the rough
order of a few hundred million dollars a year in equity-method income FIS gave
up; this is a rough approximation from eight days of data, not a disclosed
annual figure, and the primary disclosure does not state whether the $500–700M
accretion figure is already net of this foregone income or is a gross figure.
This ambiguity is a real, unresolved underwriting question, not resolved here.

**[FACT]** Q2 2026: revenue $3.4B (+5.3% pro forma); Banking Solutions +6.1%,
margin +193bps (management attributes this partly to Issuer Solutions);
Capital Markets +3.2–3.5%, margin −32bps ("higher labor costs and timing").
**[FACT]** Balance sheet: total debt $21.2B, cash $744M → **net debt ≈$20.5B**.
**[MGMT]** FIS states it has "temporarily curtailed share repurchases" and
"expects to resume meaningful share repurchases once it has achieved its
target gross leverage of approximately 2.8x" — the current leverage multiple
is not disclosed in the release reviewed. **[FACT]** Q2 buybacks: only $42M;
H1: $119M. Dividends: $228M paid in Q2; policy is "dividend per share growth
in line with Adjusted EPS growth." **[FACT]** 517M diluted weighted-average
shares.

**Capital allocation reality check:** the "better per-share cash generation"
half of the variant thesis depends on share count reduction, and buybacks are
explicitly, currently curtailed. Any near-term per-share benefit is therefore
principally an FCF-guidance story, not yet a share-count story.

### B. What appears priced in

**[FACT]** Stock ≈$41.87 (mid-session, Sept 4); **[CTX]** down ~32.6% YTD as
of the Aug 4 print, with a >10% single-session decline that day. **[CTX]**
Forward P/E ≈6.25x versus a stated 5-year average of ≈14.0x — a large
discount. **[CTX]** EV/EBITDA ≈12.4x versus a stated 5-year average of ≈12.0x
— roughly **in line** with history. The combination (depressed P/E, normal
EV/EBITDA) is consistent with the market applying a roughly normal multiple to
EBITDA while discounting per-share earnings for elevated leverage/interest
expense and acquisition-related D&A — **[ATLAS]** this is a more precise
reading of "priced in" than a blanket "cheap stock" claim, and it is directly
supported by the two multiples moving differently.

### C. Atlas variant

> The market is pricing FIS as one undifferentiated "guidance cut" story, when
> the disclosed evidence shows two distinguishable and oppositely-moving
> segment stories in the same release: Capital Markets softness (named,
> bounded, ~30% of a much larger company) against Banking/Issuer Solutions
> execution management calls "ahead of plan" with a raised, quantified FCF
> guide. The depressed P/E relative to a roughly normal EV/EBITDA multiple is
> consistent with the market discounting per-share economics for leverage the
> company has stated a specific plan (2.8x target) to unwind.

### D. Why the gap can exist

**[ATLAS]** A single mixed disclosure — a raise (FCF, +$100M) bundled with a
cut (revenue/EPS, modest) plus a strategic-review announcement disclosed on
the earnings call rather than in the press-release text — is exactly the kind
of release that rewards headline-scanning over segment-level reading, especially
compressed into one trading session (>10% move). Elevated leverage genuinely
complicates the read: it is real and disclosed, not manufactured, and gives
the negative headline framing a legitimate anchor.

### E. Gap closer

**[ATLAS]** (1) Capital Markets segment revenue/margin trend over the next
1–2 quarters — stabilizing vs. continuing to soften. (2) Disclosed leverage
ratio progress toward the 2.8x target and any buyback-resumption announcement.
(3) Clarification, if ever given, of whether the $500–700M FCF figure is gross
or net of foregone Worldpay income.

### F. Gap horizon

**[ATLAS]** 2–4 quarters (through FY2026 close and into FY2027) — the FCF
accretion is explicitly guided through 2028, but the near-term readable signal
is Capital Markets stabilization and leverage progress, both quarterly-visible.

### G. Wrong-if / kill criteria

Capital Markets softness spreads into Banking/Issuer Solutions in a subsequent
quarter; leverage fails to progress toward 2.8x or buybacks remain curtailed
beyond FY2026; the $500–700M FCF figure is later clarified as already netted
against foregone Worldpay income (meaning the "gap" was smaller than the
headline number implied); or integration costs materially exceed what's
currently disclosed.

## MELI

### A. Business / earnings engine

**[FACT]** Two engines: commerce (net revenue +40% FX-neutral / +50% USD, aided
by a durable Brazil free-shipping conversion "step-change") and fintech/credit
(portfolio $16.06B, **+75% YoY**; revenue growth decelerated to 47% FX-neutral).
**[FACT]** Consolidated: net income $466M, **down 11% YoY** despite 50%
revenue growth. Adjusted FCF $214M in the quarter, after $441M capex and a
disclosed **$2.1B of investment in credit-portfolio expansion**. **[FACT]**
Cash $3.6B, total debt $13.2B, net debt $6.4B — the credit book is funded
through "customer deposits (via Mercado Pago), warehouse facilities, and
securitization" **[MGMT]**, with limited disclosed detail on blended funding
cost. **[FACT]** Buybacks negligible ($1M for H1); no dividend. 50.7M diluted
shares, essentially flat.

**[FACT]** Provisions for doubtful accounts $1,276M in Q2, **+85% YoY**
(vs. $690M) — outpacing the 75% portfolio growth rate. Allowance for credit
losses $4,251M (Jun 30, 2026) vs. $3,057M (Dec 31, 2025). **[FACT]** NIMAL
(net interest margin after losses) 20.7%, −2ppts YoY; **credit-card NIMAL
specifically moved from breakeven to −2.5% YoY.** **[MGMT]** attributed to
"new cohort dilution" from accelerated card issuance (2.6M new cards in Q2'26
vs. 1.6M in Q2'25) that "matures" per historical pattern, and management states
explicitly this does not reflect "deterioration in asset quality."

### B. What appears priced in

**[FACT]** Stock ≈$1,977.79 (mid-session, Sept 4). **[CTX]** Forward P/E
≈41.7x — a 5-year-average comparison of ≈179x appears distorted by a
historical low-earnings period and is not treated as meaningful context here.
**[CTX]** EV/EBITDA ≈23.7–25.0x versus a cited 10-year median of ≈45x — a
material discount on this metric specifically, consistent with the market
discounting current profitability given the disclosed margin compression and
now, per this record's own finding, the net-income decline.

### C. Atlas variant

> The prevailing narrative (50% revenue growth, 15–90 day NPL "near historic
> lows") is favorable and is the figure most visibly cited. The primary
> evidence supports a **more specific and directionally cautionary** claim:
> **net income fell 11% YoY, provisions grew faster (85%) than the credit
> book (75%), and credit-card NIMAL swung from breakeven to −2.5% — a
> loss-adjusted profitability deterioration the headline NPL metric does not
> capture.** This is not "credit is growing rapidly but losses might rise";
> it is a specific claim that a real, currently-observable profitability
> deterioration (not merely a future risk) is understated by the headline
> framing.

**This variant, as evidenced, points toward caution rather than
undiscovered upside.** Pilot 2's own design permits a negative variant; this
record states it as found, rather than reframing it as bullish to justify
advancement.

### D. Why the gap can exist

**[ATLAS]** NPL is the simpler, more commonly cited credit metric; NIMAL and
the provisioning growth-rate delta require reading further into the earnings
materials. A consolidated 50%-revenue-growth headline is also a more
attention-grabbing number than an 11%-net-income-decline buried beneath it.

### E. Gap closer

**[ATLAS]** Whether credit-card NIMAL recovers toward breakeven/positive over
the next 2–3 quarters as new cohorts mature (management's own predicted
mechanism), without a further step-up in provisioning intensity relative to
portfolio growth.

### F. Gap horizon

**[ATLAS]** 2–3 quarters — management's own "cohorts mature" explanation is
directly testable on that timeframe.

### G. Wrong-if / kill criteria

Credit-card NIMAL recovers toward breakeven within 2–3 quarters without
further provisioning acceleration; net income growth resumes in line with
revenue growth; the 15–90 day NPL metric remains near historic lows through
the cohort-maturation window — any of these would support the "credit quality
is fine, NPL is the right lens" framing over this record's cautionary variant.

> the core, excluding New Businesses entirely — itself declined 4.7% YoY, led
> by an 11.8% drop in electronics/appliances, JD's historical flagship
> category.** Management's explanation ("high base effect") is specific and
> falsifiable: if correct, JD Retail growth should visibly reaccelerate once
> the comparison laps, without further deterioration concentrated in

### D. Why the gap can exist

**[ATLAS]** A round consolidated profit headline is simpler to report and
react to than the underlying segment math; "high base effect" is management's
own claim, not yet checked against independent category-level competitive
data (share trends versus Alibaba/Pinduoduo/Douyin — notably, **this specific
release contains no competitive commentary at all**, a real evidence gap for
the next stage).

### E. Gap closer

**[ATLAS]** JD Retail revenue trend over the next 1–2 quarters, specifically
whether electronics/appliances stabilizes, and whether the pace of decline
matches what a pure comparison/base effect would predict.

### F. Gap horizon

**[ATLAS]** 2–3 quarters — a base-effect explanation should resolve within
roughly a year of the cited comparison period.

### G. Wrong-if / kill criteria

JD Retail revenue continues declining beyond the quarter(s) the base-effect
explanation would predict, with electronics/appliances specifically showing
no stabilization; buyback pace slows materially (suggesting management itself
is preserving cash defensively rather than confidently); or independent
category-share data (not found in this release) shows continued share loss to

**A search for any resolution of the capital raise, through early September

determines most of the forward return distribution, and it is genuinely
unresolved as of this record.

### B. What appears priced in

**[FACT]** Stock ≈$52.09 (mid-session, Sept 4; a search-cited $52.45 close on
Sept 1 is consistent). **[ATLAS]** ≈103M shares → market cap ≈$5.4B; against
net debt ≈$1.77B, **EV ≈$7.15B**. **[CTX]** Financial press coverage of the
same period is uniformly framed as growth-confirming ("Golar LNG Greenlights
Fourth FLNG as Q2 2026 Earnings Surge," "A New Era of Growth," "Golar presses
ahead with fourth FLNG unit") — implicitly treating both newbuilds, and the
disclosed ~$800M 2028 run-rate EBITDA target that depends on them, as
effectively confirmed.

### C. Atlas variant

> Press coverage treats the fourth-FLNG order as a confirmed growth catalyst.
> The primary filing discloses it as **explicitly contingent and time-bound**:
> no end-user contract, and a management-stated possibility of EPC termination
> absent a capital raise **management itself dates to before the end of
> Q3 2026.** The growth-story narrative and the disclosed financing risk are
> not the same claim, and the gap between them is real and specific.

### D. Why the gap can exist

**[ATLAS]** The financing contingency sits in interim-report liquidity/risk-
factor language, not the order-announcement headlines that dominate general
coverage. A signed EPC with a named yard (CIMC Raffles) reads, on its face,
like committed growth; the financing dependency and termination clause require
reading the risk-factor and liquidity sections specifically.

### E. Gap closer

**[FACT/pending]** A subsequent 6-K or press release confirming either (a) the
long-term asset-level/corporate financing referenced in the H1 2026 report has
been secured, or (b) the EPC for the fourth unit has been terminated per the
disclosed contingency. **This is the single most decision-relevant fact
outstanding for this name.**

### F. Gap horizon

**Immediate — within weeks, not quarters.** Management's own disclosed window
is "before the end of Q3 2026," concurrent with this record's date.

### G. Wrong-if / kill criteria

The variant (a specific, resolvable financing-risk gap) is invalidated in
either direction once the outcome is known: if financing succeeds, the risk
this record flags is resolved favorably and the growth narrative is
validated; if the EPC is terminated, the ~$800M 2028 run-rate target embedded
in the "new era of growth" framing does not hold as disclosed and needs
re-underwriting from the two confirmed assets (Gimi, Hilli) alone.

### Why GLNG does not receive a valuation/return range in Section 7

**[ATLAS]** A defensible base-case return distribution cannot be built around
a live, binary, company-disclosed near-term contingency without effectively
picking a side. Assuming successful financing would adopt the bullish press
narrative uncritically; assuming failure would be equally unfounded
pessimism. This is recorded as `BLOCKED_BY_MATERIAL_UNCERTAINTY`, not
`NO_DEMONSTRATED_EDGE` — the variant is specific and well-evidenced; it is the
underlying outcome, not the underwriting, that is unresolved.
