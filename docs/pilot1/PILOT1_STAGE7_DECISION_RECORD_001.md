Seven members, as assembled for this comparison:

| Treasury / Cash Sleeve | Cash-management governance object |
| DO NOTHING / status quo | Governance object |

**SPY is labeled beta, not alpha.** It is a broad market exposure and is
not an underwritten Atlas candidate. It never becomes an alpha candidate by
appearing in an Opportunity Set.

**Treasury / Cash Sleeve and DO NOTHING are separate governance objects.**
They are not merged, aliased, or treated as a single option.

### DO NOTHING / STATUS QUO

No new active mandate and no change to the book.

### TREASURY / CASH SLEEVE

Incremental capital is accepted into the book and deliberately assigned to
a reversible, near-risk-free parking sleeve while awaiting a sufficiently
attractive future opportunity.

### Why both, when the economics may look similar

For this fictional increment the two may produce broadly similar near-term
economics. They remain **different governance decisions**. DO NOTHING
declines to take the capital into the book under any new mandate. The
Treasury / Cash Sleeve accepts the capital into the book and assigns it a
deliberate, reversible holding role with an expectation of future
redeployment. Collapsing them would hide a real governance distinction
behind an economic coincidence, so Pilot 1 keeps them separate.

## Treasury Implementation

**3-month U.S. Treasury bill / cash equivalent as of September 1, 2026,
approximately 3.9%.**

> **This is labeled: CASH-MANAGEMENT IMPLEMENTATION.**

This figure is an implementation detail of the cash sleeve. It is
explicitly **not** a hurdle rate, and this record does not use it as one.

### Constraints on the Treasury comparator

- **Treasury maturity is not a frozen Atlas constant.** The 3-month
  selection applies to this comparison only and does not bind any future
  comparison.
- **Future Treasury maturity must be selected point-in-time**, based on the
  expected idle period, liquidity and optionality requirements, prevailing
  yields, and the likely redeployment horizon for the capital in question.
- **Treasury is a competing use of capital, not a valuation benchmark.** It
  occupies a slot in the Opportunity Set. It does not measure, discount, or
  validate any equity.
- **Yield metrics are valuation context only.** Earnings yield, FCF yield,
  AFFO yield, and dividend yield may appear as valuation context and must
  **not** be differenced against the Treasury rate as a spread purporting
  to demonstrate equity edge. No such spread is computed anywhere in this
  record, and none may be inferred from it.
