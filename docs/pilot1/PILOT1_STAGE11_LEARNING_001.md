## Lesson 1 — Refusing deployment is a capability

Pilot 1 demonstrated that Atlas will not force a buy simply because names
survived underwriting. That is a real capability and it is worth keeping.

**Pilot 2 must also prove Atlas can recognize when deployment IS earned.**

A method that can only decline is only half a method. The two capabilities
are distinct, and only one of them has been shown.

---

## Lesson 2 — "What is priced in?" is the highest-priority methodology gap

This remains an OPEN METHODOLOGY QUESTION
(`../manual_pilot_v0_1/01_DISCOVERY_CONTRACT.md`) and Pilot 1 confirmed it
is the binding constraint on decision edge.

**Pilot 2 gets a minimum, human-readable Priced-In / Variant View
framework.**

Explicitly excluded:

- No estimate vendor.
- No large reverse-DCF system.
- No precise expected-return model.

---

## Lesson 3 — MATERIAL UNCERTAINTY needs typed, non-scored categories

A single undifferentiated ATTRACTIVE WITH MATERIAL UNCERTAINTY conclusion
carried four names into Stage 7 indistinguishable from one another. The
conclusion needs type.

**Categories (non-scored):**

- valuation / expectations
- demand / cyclical
- competitive / structural
- rates / financing
- execution / capital allocation
- governance / management
- evidence / data

**Pilot 2 must also ask:**

- Does the uncertainty **block** an increment?
- Or does it merely **cap size**?
- **What fact would resolve it?**

The block-vs-cap distinction is the operative addition. It converts
uncertainty from a uniform disqualifier into something that can produce
different treatments for different names.

## Lesson 4 — Pilot 2 Stage 7 uses a fixed qualitative, non-scored grid

Rows, answered in plain language:

- named edge vs cash / status quo
- named edge vs beta
- uncertainty type
- portfolio factor overlap
- liquidity / reversibility
- relevant time horizon
- falsification condition
- funding source

**Constraints:**

- No weights.
- No composite.
- No optimizer.
- No automatic winner.

The grid organizes human judgment for legibility and comparability. It does
not compute a result, and no cell or combination of cells selects an
alternative.

---

## Lesson 5 — Funding source must be explicit

Every Decision Record must name one of:

- new external capital
- existing Treasury / cash sleeve
- proceeds from reducing / exiting a holding

These are materially different capital questions and must not be
conflated.

---

## Lesson 6 — Treasury / Cash Sleeve remains idle-capital management

Maturity is selected point-in-time based on optionality, liquidity,
expected idle period, prevailing yields, and likely redeployment horizon.

> **Treasury is not a frozen Atlas hurdle rate.**

Neither the maturity nor the yield used in any single record becomes a
constant of the method. Treasury is a competing use of capital, never a
valuation benchmark and never a threshold an equity must clear.

---

## Lesson 7 — Red Team value

The two-pass Red Team process materially improved capital-decision
discipline by catching:

- premature sizing
- unsupported ranking
- false equity-yield vs Treasury comparisons
- portfolio-fit over-inference
- the missing market-expectations framework

**Keep two Red Team passes per pilot, around Stage 7 / remediation, before
Stage 8.**

This is a process retention, not a new system. It adds no infrastructure.
